\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Options markets generate large and rapidly changing collections of quotes across strikes and maturities. Cboe Global Markets reported that 4.6 billion contracts were traded across its four options exchanges in 2025, the sixth consecutive annual record. S\&P 500 Index (SPX) options accounted for 970.6 million contracts, with an average daily volume of 3.9 million \cite{cboe2025report}. At this scale, constructing implied volatility (IV) surfaces efficiently from irregular market data is practically important for pricing and risk measurement.

Implied volatility is the volatility parameter that reproduces a quoted option price in the Black-Scholes model. Collecting these values across strikes and maturities gives an implied volatility surface for a particular market snapshot. The surface is not observed continuously: market quotes are available only at a discrete and changing set of locations and may contain noise or temporary inconsistencies. A usable estimate must therefore smooth the observations while retaining the financial structure required of an option-price surface \cite{gonon2024operator}.

Traditional parametric approaches, including the Stochastic Volatility Inspired (SVI) model \cite{gatheral2014ssvi}, fit a prescribed functional form to each new snapshot. This generally requires a separate numerical optimisation whenever the available quotes change. Gonon, Jacquier, and Wiedemann \cite{gonon2024operator} instead formulate implied volatility smoothing as an operator-learning problem. Their Graph Neural Operator (GNO) accepts observations at irregular locations and produces a smooth surface in one forward pass. It can process changing input configurations without first interpolating every snapshot onto a fixed grid, and its training objective includes penalties for relevant financial shape conditions.

The trained GNO nevertheless returns a deterministic point estimate. In this thesis, uncertainty refers to the discrepancy between that estimate and the market implied volatility at quote locations withheld from the GNO input. The resulting bands describe uncertainty around the reconstructed surface at observed locations; they are not forecasts of future realised volatility or confidence intervals for the GNO parameters. This distinction is important because prediction errors, quote density, liquidity, and option sensitivity differ across the surface.

Monte Carlo dropout and deep ensembles can quantify uncertainty, but they require repeated stochastic evaluations or multiple trained models \cite{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017simple}. Conformal prediction offers a post-hoc alternative that calibrates prediction errors from a fixed model. Under exchangeability of the calibration and test observations, the standard construction provides finite-sample marginal coverage without imposing a parametric error distribution \cite{shafer2008tutorial,angelopoulos2023gentle}. The data are ordered over time, and quotes from the same market surface are related. Therefore, the exchangeability assumption required for the standard conformal guarantee may not hold. This thesis reports empirical coverage from a walk-forward evaluation instead of claiming a formal coverage guarantee.

Applying conformal prediction in this setting requires a score that reflects the heterogeneous scale of errors across the option domain. This thesis compares a price residual normalised by the bid and ask spread with an implied volatility residual weighted by Vega. Related research has studied conformal prediction for operator learning in scientific applications \cite{ma2024calibrated} and has used implied volatility as a predictor of future realised volatility \cite{canete2023market}. The literature reviewed in Chapter~\ref{chap:literature}, however, does not address post-hoc conformal bands around a GNO-smoothed implied volatility surface constructed from irregular option quotes. This is the gap examined in the remainder of the thesis.

\section{Motivation and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:motivation}

The central problem addressed in this thesis is the construction of calibrated uncertainty bands for implied volatility surfaces produced by the Graph Neural Operator approach. More specifically, this work develops an efficient uncertainty quantification layer that:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Targets finite-sample marginal coverage across observed surface points under exchangeability, without requiring a parametric model for the prediction errors;
    \item Accounts for heteroskedastic prediction errors through economically motivated normalisation;
    \item Responds to changing market conditions through rolling calibration windows;
    \item Produces uncertainty bands in both implied volatility and price spaces; and
    \item Applies a post-processing routine that enforces a calendar condition and a discrete butterfly condition based on neighbouring grid points on the lower and upper band surfaces.
\end{enumerate}

Options data are heterogeneous across both time and the strike-expiry domain. Liquidity, quote density, bid-ask spreads, and price sensitivity vary between regions of the surface. The proposed framework therefore allows the band width to vary across observed points and evaluates whether spatially adaptive calibration improves coverage across the surface while retaining marginal evaluation over all observations.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

This thesis pursues the following specific objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Design economically motivated nonconformity scores:} Develop two heteroskedastic scoring mechanisms that scale prediction errors using (a) bid-ask spreads in price space and (b) Vega-based weights in implied volatility space, so that uncertainty bands reflect trading frictions and option sensitivity.

    \item \textbf{Implement rolling window calibration:} Establish a temporal adaptation mechanism using a sliding window of recent market snapshots to compute conformal quantiles, enabling the framework to track regime changes in market volatility dynamics.

    \item \textbf{Evaluate spatial heterogeneity via Mondrian conformal prediction:} Investigate whether partitioning the strike-expiry domain into spatial bins and computing region-specific quantiles improves coverage uniformity across areas with varying liquidity.

    \item \textbf{Construct prediction bands in dual spaces:} Provide practitioners with uncertainty estimates in both implied volatility space (for volatility trading) and price space (for delta hedging and risk management).

    \item \textbf{Apply calendar and discrete butterfly adjustments to prediction bands:} Apply a projection algorithm to the lower and upper band surfaces and evaluate the calendar and discrete butterfly conditions on the finite $(\rho,z)$ grid used by the implementation.

    \item \textbf{Validate empirical coverage:} Assess whether the conformal band surfaces achieve the nominal marginal coverage probability $(1 - \alpha)$ across observed quote locations in S\&P 500 options data spanning December 2025 to August 2026.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The main contributions of this thesis are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First application of conformal prediction to neural operator-based volatility surfaces:} While conformal prediction has been applied to operator learning in scientific computing \cite{ma2024calibrated}, and to implied volatility forecasting without neural operators \cite{canete2023market}, to the best of our knowledge this work represents the first integration of distribution-free uncertainty quantification with operator learning architectures specifically in the context of implied volatility smoothing.

    \item \textbf{Economically aligned nonconformity scores:} Adaptation of spread-normalised price errors and Vega-weighted implied volatility errors for conformal calibration around the GNO surface. These constructions incorporate bid-ask spread information and option sensitivity when determining the band widths.

    \item \textbf{Temporal adaptation via rolling calibration:} Use of a rolling window of recent calibration data so that the conformal quantiles respond to changes in the observed prediction errors. Its performance on the temporally ordered surfaces is assessed empirically through the walk-forward evaluation.

    \item \textbf{Grid-based projection of uncertainty bands:} Implementation of a procedure that applies the calendar and discrete butterfly adjustments used in this thesis to both the lower and upper band surfaces, together with diagnostics for residual violations and lower-upper crossings.

    \item \textbf{Empirical validation on real market data:} Comprehensive evaluation on S\&P 500 options data spanning December 2025 to August 2026, providing evidence that conformal prediction can deliver empirically calibrated uncertainty surfaces in a challenging real-world financial application.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Outline}
\label{sec:outline}

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows:

\textbf{Chapter 2: Background and Related Work} provides the necessary background on implied volatility surfaces, no-arbitrage constraints, traditional smoothing methods, neural operators, and the theoretical foundations of conformal prediction. We review related work on uncertainty quantification in financial machine learning and position our contribution within this literature.

\textbf{Chapter 3: Methodology} presents the technical details of our conformal uncertainty quantification framework. We formally define the problem, introduce the spread-normalised and Vega-weighted nonconformity scores, describe the rolling window calibration mechanism, explain Mondrian conformal prediction for spatial heterogeneity, detail the construction of prediction bands in both price and implied volatility spaces, and present the grid-based projection procedure used to apply calendar and discrete butterfly adjustments to the resulting band surfaces.

\textbf{Chapter 4: Implementation} describes the software architecture, data preprocessing pipeline, and integration with the pre-trained GNO model from Gonon et al.

\textbf{Chapter 5: Experiments and Results} reports the empirical evaluation on S\&P 500 options data. We present coverage and sharpness results, visualisations of the uncertainty bands, the comparison of Score A and Score B, the comparison of global and Mondrian calibration, and the projection diagnostics.

\textbf{Chapter 6: Discussion} interprets the results, examines the practical implications for risk management and trading, discusses the limitations of the approach (particularly regarding the exchangeability assumption), and considers the financial validity of the uncertainty bands.

\textbf{Chapter 7: Conclusion} summarises the contributions, reflects on the broader significance of distribution-free uncertainty quantification for financial machine learning, and outlines directions for future work, including extensions to online conformal prediction, adaptive spatial binning, and integration with richer option pricing models.
